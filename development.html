Personal development

Reflection on The Richest Man in Babylon by George S. Clason.

  • Seven Cures for a Lean Purse
  • Five Laws of Gold
  • Biggest takeaway

Reading The Richest Man in Babylon by George S. Clason connected with me in a way I did not expect because some of the lessons in the book reminded me of what I had already been doing in my own life.

Recently, I came off working and travelling, and for months my main focus was saving money. I was not trying to spend just because I had money in my pocket. I had goals that were bigger than the temporary things I could buy, so I made the decision to minimize my spending and put away as much as I could. There were times when I wanted to buy certain things or enjoy my money, but I had to remind myself that I was working for a bigger purpose.

Seven Cures for a Lean Purse

That experience made the chapter “Seven Cures for a Lean Purse” feel very real to me. The first lesson is to pay yourself first by saving a portion of what you earn. Looking back, that was exactly what I was trying to do. After working and travelling, instead of coming home and spending everything I earned, I made saving a priority. For months, I focused on keeping my expenses low and holding on to my money.

I realized that saving money is not always easy, especially when you are young and have things you want. Sometimes you see something you could buy and think, I worked for this, so why not spend it? But I learned that just because I can afford something does not mean I need it. I had to learn the difference between something I wanted and something I actually needed.

That connects directly to the book’s lesson about controlling your expenditures. I learned this lesson through experience before I even read about it. I had to minimize unnecessary spending because I knew that if I spent everything I earned, I would eventually have nothing to show for all the work I had done.

Coming off work and travelling also made me appreciate how hard money can be to earn. When you have to put in long hours and make sacrifices to earn your money, you start looking at every dollar differently. I did not want months of work to disappear in a few weeks because I was careless with my spending. I wanted to look at my savings and know that the sacrifices I made were actually leading somewhere.

Five Laws of Gold

The chapter “Five Laws of Gold” also made me think about what I should do with the money I managed to save. Saving is important, but I now understand that simply keeping money is not the entire goal. Eventually, I want to learn how to make my money grow. However, I also learned that I should not rush into investments or opportunities just because they promise quick money. I need to understand what I am doing and make informed decisions.

This is especially important to me because I am hungry for money and success. I want to be financially successful, but I do not want to become desperate and make reckless decisions. I want to build my money gradually and intelligently. The book taught me that protecting the money I have worked hard for is just as important as making more of it.

My experience working and travelling also changed the way I think about success. I have seen financial struggles in my family, and I know that I do not want to repeat the same patterns. I want to create a different future for myself. I have always believed that I need to be willing to work for what I want and not depend on somebody else to make things happen for me.

For months, I saved and minimized my spending because I had a goal in mind. There was nothing glamorous about it. It meant saying no to certain things, being careful with my money, and reminding myself why I was working in the first place. But looking back, I realize that those sacrifices were teaching me something important: discipline today can create freedom tomorrow.

My biggest takeaway

The biggest lesson I took from The Richest Man in Babylon is that becoming wealthy starts with developing the right habits. You cannot simply want money and expect it to stay in your hands. You have to know how to earn it, save it, control your spending, protect it, and eventually make it grow.

For me, this book confirmed something I had already started learning through my own experience. I do not want to work hard just to spend everything I earn. I want my work to mean something. I want the money I earn today to help build the life I want tomorrow.

As a young Jamaican student, I am hungry for success. I know where I am starting from, and I have seen the struggles that can come with not having enough money. But I do not want my family’s struggles to determine my future. Instead, I want them to motivate me to work harder and think differently.

Coming off work and travelling, then spending months saving and cutting down on unnecessary expenses, showed me that I am capable of discipline. Reading this book helped me understand why that discipline matters. I realized that I was already applying some of the principles of the book without even knowing it.

My biggest takeaway is simple: I have to treat every dollar I earn as a tool for building my future, not just as money available to spend today. I am willing to work, save, learn, sacrifice, and keep trying. I may not have everything I want right now, but I believe that if I stay disciplined and continue improving myself, I can eventually build the success and financial independence that I am working toward.
